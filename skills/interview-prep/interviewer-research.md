# Interviewer Research

Research the **person**, not just the company. The company tells you what to say; the interviewer tells you how the next thirty minutes will actually run.

Start from what the invite already gives you: name, title, and the calendar tool. Then search.

## Where to Look

Run these as WebSearch queries. Two or three good hits beat ten shallow ones — stop when the archetype is clear.

- `"<Name>" <Company> LinkedIn` — current title, tenure at this company, the job right before this one.
- `"<Name>" <Company>` — talks, podcasts, blog posts, press quotes, launch announcements.
- Company **team / about / leadership** page — for founders and small companies this is often richer than LinkedIn.
- GitHub / personal site — only if they are an engineer and only if it is trivially findable. Do not deep-dive someone's side projects; it reads as surveillance if it surfaces.
- The **scheduling email itself** — a title that differs from the posting is a signal in its own right (see below).

If nothing is findable, say so in the doc and infer from the title alone. **Never fabricate a background.** A wrong guess said out loud is worse than no research.

## What the Background Predicts

Read the prior employer and tenure, not just the current title.

| Signal | What it predicts |
|---|---|
| **Agency-recruiting background** (staffing firm, then in-house) | Brisk, friendly, logistics-first. Comp and availability will come up early — likely from them. Will not quiz on the stack. Optimize for the one-line story they repeat to the HM. |
| **In-house / technical recruiter, long tenure** | Knows the team and the level ladder. Worth asking why the req exists, and who it reports to. |
| **Founder / CEO / CTO at a small company** | This call is most of the decision. Expect values, ownership, and "what are you improving." Product opinions land; ticket-taking does not. |
| **Hiring manager** | Scope and ownership stories, plus how you handle disagreement. Have the end-to-end feature story ready with its strongest hard number. |
| **IC peer / future teammate** | Craft-level and collaboration. Most likely to catch a bluff. This is where honest gap lines pay for themselves. |
| **Interviewer came from a competitor** | They know the domain cold. Precision matters more than framing. |
| **Anything in their public writing** | One specific, non-flattering reference to it is the strongest homework card you have. Do not force it in. |

## What Goes in the Doc

A short **Interviewer DNA** block per person, near the top, right after the frame:

- Name, title, tenure, the notable prior role — one line, `[CERTAIN]` if from their own profile, `[LIKELY]` if inferred.
- **What they will probably ask.** One sentence.
- **What they will probably not ask.** Equally useful — it tells them what to stop worrying about.
- One thing to reference that shows homework, if a real one exists. If not, leave it out rather than reaching.

For panels and multi-person calls, one block per person, plus **per-person questions** — asking the CTO and the recruiter the same question wastes both.

## Named Formats

If the invite names a format, look it up and script to it. It is the highest-leverage twenty minutes of prep available.

- **Topgrading / chronological** (from *Who: The A Method for Hiring*) — per role: why joined, hired to do, accomplished, enjoyed most/least, why moved on. Interviewers listen for **patterns across roles**: do accomplishments carry numbers, are the exits clean, does the candidate badmouth anyone. Write the chronology out per role in advance; mark the answers only the user can supply so they do not improvise them live.
- **Take-home / proctored assessment** — a different job entirely. Work out what is actually being graded before planning any build.
- **Pair programming, no-AI, in-IDE** — say so plainly and start drills before the greenlight, not after.
- **Values / behavioral questionnaire** — read the candidate guide fully if they sent one. Skimming it has cost real answers.

## Boundaries

- Public professional information only: LinkedIn, company pages, conference talks, published writing, public repos.
- No personal social accounts, home details, family, or anything the user would be uncomfortable admitting they looked at.
- **The test:** if the interviewer heard exactly how you researched them, would it read as diligence or as creepy? Anything failing that test does not go in the doc.
